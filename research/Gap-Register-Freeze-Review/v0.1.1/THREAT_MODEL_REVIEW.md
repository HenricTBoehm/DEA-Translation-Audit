# SR-1 threat-model review

SR-1 is a defensible **chosen policy family** for content integrity of selected high-impact decisions. It forbids the same effective principal on two material paths, one ending in production of the sealed output and one ending in the evaluation actually used. It is stricter than signer-role separation. The study must declare whether its design is estimating conformance to this deliberately chosen policy or measuring improved protection against an independently stated material loss. Agreement with the policy cannot by itself establish a novel benefit.

## Operational basis needed before freeze

- Use canonical principals and actual key validity at the act. Separate actor-of-record, authorized credential, effective control and independent evidence issuer.
- Give the production side an evidenced route to alter an admissible part of the evaluated output before the exact seal. The actor having signed or run a fixed operation is not itself that route. Hold ordinary disposition authorization fixed or record it as a separate gate.
- A relevant evaluation path requires a consumed role, exact content/version and a field or effect within the selected procedure. Define material as the ability under an authorized alternative to alter eligibility, output validity, binding or the protected outcome. A realized change is unnecessary. If the study instead prohibits all control of certain protected fields even when no outcome could change, state that stronger integrity policy and score it separately.
- Give reads and dynamic resolution their own evidence. Bind what was actually consumed to the approved role, scope and attempt. The ability to enumerate reported files does not attest all runtime accesses. A complete inventory is a trust premise; a signature authenticates the assertion to an issuer, without establishing its truth.
- `modify` creates or contributes to a committed snapshot with its own content identity. Later key revocation changes future credential acceptance under a stated validation time and policy. It does not undo an earlier artifact effect. A reset needs a separately evidenced effect, exact version, channel, proof of authority and observed success. Changing frozen content requires a successor version and a permitted new binding. Credential reset alone does not neutralize a criteria effect. The frozen output, evidence snapshot and actual commit scope must agree.
